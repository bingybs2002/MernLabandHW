# Roles

### 1. What is the difference between Agile and Waterfall methodologies?

Waterfall is sequential: requirements, design, development, testing, then deployment, with each phase finishing before the next. It suits fixed, well-understood scope, but changes are costly and feedback comes late. Agile is iterative: work is delivered in short iterations with continuous customer feedback, so it handles changing requirements and delivers working software early. Agile favors collaboration and adaptability, while Waterfall favors upfront planning and documentation.

- **Agile vs Scrum?**  
  Agile is a mindset defined by the Agile Manifesto's values and principles (individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration, responding to change). Scrum is one specific framework for practicing Agile, with fixed-length sprints, defined roles (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers), events, and artifacts (product backlog, sprint backlog, increment). Kanban is another Agile approach without sprints. So all Scrum is Agile, but not all Agile is Scrum.

### 2. What are some common roles in scrum and what they do?

Scrum officially defines three accountabilities: Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers. Teams also commonly work with other roles that sit outside Scrum itself.

- **PO, PM, devs, etc**  
  Product Owner: owns product priorities, manages and orders the backlog, writes or approves user stories and acceptance criteria, and maximizes product value. Scrum Master: facilitates Scrum events, removes impediments, coaches the team, and protects it from distractions. Developers: the cross-functional people who build, test, and deliver the increment each sprint (including QA). Project Manager: not a Scrum role, but common in companies to coordinate scope, schedule, budget, resources, risks, and stakeholders across teams. Others: tech lead/architect, designers, and engineering manager.

### 3. Who are some common people that developers have to interact often with?

Common interactions include other developers, tech leads/architects, Product Owners, Scrum Masters, QA engineers, UX/UI designers, DevOps/SRE, database engineers, security teams, and business stakeholders. The exact mix depends on the organization.

- **Who are the stakeholders?**  
  Stakeholders are people or groups affected by or interested in the product or project. Examples include customers, end users, product owners, business leaders, managers, developers, QA, operations, security, compliance, and sometimes external vendors or clients.
